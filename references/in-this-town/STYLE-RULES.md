# "In This Town" style rules

What this file is: the result of studying 5 viral "In This Town" blocky-3D story videos frame by frame (cut detection, word-timed transcripts, contact sheets every 3s, loudness).
What it's for: the rules every episode we make has to follow.

The tutorial (tiktok2, get_rich_with_david) gives the same formula: short, one weird rule, simple colorful characters, word-by-word captions, a twist at the end.

## 1. Story formula

1. **Line 1 is always the hook: "In this town, <one strange rule>."** The rule is spoken at 0.0s, with no intro and no title card.
2. **Next 3–5 lines show the rule with examples around town (worldbuilding).** Each example is concrete and visual, and often funny: the teacher writes "LAVA" instead of "math", grandma tries ordering a baby mammoth, "even the pigeons left". Use exactly one shot per example.
3. **Then introduce "me".** The narrator is a kid in the first person, and the exception or underdog of the rule: "And me? Well, this is my house." / "...except me."
4. **Add personal details that will pay off later.** tiktok1 plants clues (she never shows the selfies; mom only answers "Sure, sweetheart"; the dog follows me) and every one of them comes back in the twist.
5. **Inciting event.** Pin it to a time or place: "Last night… 2:13 a.m.", "one morning", "on Saturday".
6. **Escalation with sound-word beats:** "Boom!", "Click!", "Hush, hush, hush". Dialogue is short and in quotes, read by the same narrator.
7. **Twist or cliffhanger in the last 10–15s.** Either it re-frames everything (tiktok1: "I never had one. I am one.") or it ends on an open threat ("Because if those lights go out again, he's dead." / "the previous owner did not fall. He was pushed."), sometimes with "Part 2 soon" in the caption.
8. **The last line echoes the rule or the first line,** for a satisfying loop that drives rewatches: "Now everyone's playing basketball… except me."

**Length:** 60–135s. A 30–45s short version only works with a single example and a fast twist.
**Script size:** 245–480 words. Write about 2.8 words per second of target length.
**Sentence style:** short. Many sentences are 3–8 words, and fragments are fine ("Game day." "Number 12." "Still alive.").

## 2. Voice

- **Pace:** fast. 165–220 words/min, with no dead air between lines (aim for 0.1–0.25s gaps, not 0.5s).
- **Voice:** one narrator voice does everything, including the other characters' quoted lines, with a little acting. It's an energetic, slightly dramatic storyteller, never monotone.
- **Loudness:** the mix sits around -15 to -23 dB mean. The voice is clearly on top, and music sits about 18–22 dB under it.

## 3. Visual style

- **Characters:** blocky, Roblox-like proportions (box torso, box arms and legs, big round or cylinder head). Faces are simple 2D cartoon faces: dot or oval eyes with a highlight, and expressive brows. Expressions change every few shots (neutral, smile, shock 😮, crying with blue tear streams, angry brows). Clothes have one or two bold colors plus one identifying prop (cap, chef hat, police cap, walkie-talkie, controller, gold top hat).
- **Each character has one signature outfit** and keeps it the whole video, so viewers recognize them instantly in cuts that last under 1s.
- **Crowds:** big crowds of tiny colorful characters (stands, plazas) sell scale.
- **Color:** saturated and bright, with a clean blue sky, white puffy clouds and green grass. Night scenes use deep blue with warm window lights and white floodlights. Avoid muddy or desaturated looks except for deliberate moments (a grey or sad character, the dark-city moment).
- **Lighting:** soft shadows and gentle ambient occlusion. Warm "golden hour" light is for emotional stories (tiktok1); flat bright daylight is for comedy (tiktok6).
- **World:** modular town pieces: streets with lane markings, suburban houses, shops, a school classroom, a fountain plaza, a city hall, a basketball court, skyscrapers, living rooms. Props tell the joke: a scoreboard with numbers, a phone screen, a TV, a $999 price tag, a "WARNING" screen, a "2:13 AM" clock.
- **On-screen props carry text:** scoreboards, signs, screens and clocks show story info, so the viewer reads it without extra captions.

## 4. Camera and editing

- **Cut fast.** The median shot is 1.5–2.0s for drama and about 1.0s for comedy, with a maximum of about 5s. Never hold a shot for a whole sentence; cut on every new idea or noun ("the baker" → baker shot, "the mailman" → mailman shot).
- **Every spoken noun gets its own picture.** A shot shows exactly what the current words say.
- **Shot types, in rotation:**
  - extreme low-angle hero shots looking up at towers or monsters
  - close-up face reactions, framed so the head fills a third to half of the frame
  - over-the-shoulder shots
  - POV shots: hands holding a phone or key
  - wide establishing shots of the town or plaza
  - top-down aerial view of the town
  - object inserts: clock, radio, key, controller, screen
- **Every shot moves:** a slow push-in, orbit or drift. Character motion, such as a walk cycle, a wave or a head turn, is constant.
- **Hard cuts** are the default. A white flash goes on magical moments, a quick shake on impacts ("Boom!"), and a fade to black only at the very end.
- **Effects:** sparks, rain streaks, light beams, glowing particles, explosions in simple stylized form.

## 5. Captions (the most copied element)

- **Show exactly one word at a time,** synced to the voice, and switch on every word. Numbers stay as digits ("2:13am", "200", "6").
- **Position:** center screen, about 55–62% of the height (just below the middle). This is the same spot every time.
- **Font:** a heavy rounded sans (Poppins/Fredoka-like, weight 800).
- **Two observed color styles:**
  - **Lime yellow** (`#E8FF3A`-ish) fill with a thick dark outline (zaylento). This is the strongest "In This Town" look.
  - **White** fill with a black outline and a soft shadow (whatifstories, synelite).
- **Size:** about 70–90px at 1080 wide for normal words. **Emphasis words get bigger** (about 1.4–1.6×: "literally", "massive", "guards", "shining") and **filler words get smaller** (about 0.6×: "well", "inside", "everything").
- **Animation:** a tiny pop (scale 1.15 → 1.0 over about 80ms) on each word. There's no karaoke highlight and no full sentences.

## 6. Audio

- **Music:** a light background track under the whole video (playful for comedy, soft piano or tension for drama) that ducks under the voice.
- **Sound effects** for every action word: whoosh on cuts and moves, boom, click, radio crackle, crowd cheer, ball bounce, thunder. Sound-word lines ("Boom!", "Swish") always get a real sound effect.

## 7. Format

- 1080×1920 vertical, 30fps, H.264.
- No intro, logo or outro card. The first frame is already the story; the last line is followed by about 1s of hold or fade.
- Hashtags in the caption: #inthistown #roblox #robloxstory #robloxanimation #shortstory
